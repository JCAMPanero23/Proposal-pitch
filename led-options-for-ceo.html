Component Architecture

The repeating 600mm grid unit

Independent of framing or density choice, every grid unit follows the same three-layer stack: a sealed substrate at the base, transparent stems anchored into it, and replaceable tulip heads on top.

Exploded view of the 600mm grid unit (16-LED variant shown).

Tulip Head — replaceable, ~2-3 yr life

Two mold variants (Closed 60% / Open 40%). Hot-swappable. Holds the addressable RGBW LED behind a UV-resistant diffuser. Adds ~40-50mm above the stem.

Stem — transparent, set on install

PC or PC/silicone hybrid. Heights 400-600mm in 50mm steps (plus tulip head on top) — that's 2/3 to 1× the block's 600mm width, so the stems clearly dominate above a flat base. Locked at commissioning. Passive flex only, no motors.

Per-cell electronics — sealed IP67

16-channel or 9-channel addressable LED driver. Sits inside the sealed block (Option C), under the open-frame cell (Option D), or in an internal cavity within the tile (Options E1/E2).

Stem height recipe (controlled-random across the field):
400mm 15% · 450mm 20% · 500mm 30% · 550mm 20% · 600mm 15%
Decision 1

Framing option

Four options compared. The earlier bulky-pad concepts (multi-cell precast / cast-in-place pads) have been replaced with two clean modular tile variants based on your sketch — same form factor as Option C's block, but flat on ground with no raised frame. Substructure cost shown is the substructure portion only — full installed system cost (which adds LEDs, electronics, walkway, install, spares) is in the cost matrix below.

Option C Raised frame + sealed lift-out blocks

100-300mm raised structural grid. Sealed 600mm aluminum blocks with integrated electronics lift out from above. Cables and drainage run in the plenum below.

Pros

  • Best in-field serviceability
  • Cables and drainage hidden in plenum
  • Original recommendation; well-developed spec

Cons

  • Raised deck creates a trip hazard
  • Sealed block is its own manufactured product (cost)
  • No longer the cleanest aerial reading
Substructure cost (mid): AED 4.6M  ·  Per module: ~AED 745  ·  Ground level: No (raised 100-300mm)

Option D Modular open-frame grid on prepared ground Recommended for pilot

Bolt-together 600mm aluminum grid sits flush on prepared ground. No raised feet, no top cover plate. Stems anchor directly into the frame. Sealed IP67 electronics puck under each cell.

Pros

  • Cleanest ground-level appearance
  • Lowest civil program — no slab pour
  • Relocatable for touring or pilots
  • Fastest install / decommission

Cons

  • Sand and dust collect in cells — needs blow-out cycle
  • Less foot-traffic protection on stems
  • Heat dissipation depends on small puck cases
Substructure cost (mid): AED 3.2M  ·  Per module: ~AED 520  ·  Ground level: Yes (flush)

Option E1 Steel plate tile, flush on ground

Same modular form factor as Option C (a sealed 600mm × 600mm tile with stems on top and the LED driver inside), but in steel instead of aluminum and laid flat on prepared ground with no raised frame. Each tile has lifting holes at the corners for crane handling. Driver inside the tile's internal cavity. Tiles set edge-to-edge like heavy paving slabs.

Pros

  • Clean ground-level appearance — no raised deck, no trip hazard
  • Familiar form factor — extends Option C, easy to spec and manufacture
  • Steel is highly durable and stable in UAE heat / dust
  • Self-contained tile — no field-side wiring per stem, only inter-tile data
  • Each tile lifts out for service — best of both worlds vs Option D

Cons

  • Steel is heavier than aluminum — bigger crane, more handling labor
  • More expensive than LWAC concrete tile (E2)
  • Each tile is its own manufactured product (cost vs Option D's bare frame)
  • Joints between tiles need a sealing/maintenance plan
Substructure cost (mid): AED 3.7M  ·  Per module: ~AED 600  ·  Ground level: Yes (flush)

Option E2 Light-weight pre-fabricated concrete tile (cast on site) Recommended for flagship

Same modular tile form factor as E1, but in lightweight aggregate concrete (LWAC) instead of steel. Tiles are batch-cast on site in simple molds, cured, and then set in place edge-to-edge on a thin sand-cement bedding. Each tile is 600mm × 600mm × 60mm with lifting holes at the corners and a recessed driver cavity on the underside. Stems mount into integral sockets on the top face.

Weight & handling: ~37 kg per tile (60mm × 1,700 kg/m³ LWAC mix). That's a single-person lift with a paving suction-cup grip — same handling envelope as a heavy garden paver. No crane needed for maintenance; a small mobile gantry only helps speed up initial install. Most service events (tulip head / stem swaps) don't lift the tile at all — only driver replacement (~5%/yr) does.

Pros

  • Cheapest substructure of all four options
  • Single-person lift at 60mm thickness — no crane for service
  • Material is locally sourced (UAE LECA suppliers) — no import
  • Batch-cast on site in simple molds — minimal civil program
  • Lower thermal mass than normal concrete — surface ~5-10°C cooler at showtime
  • Same lift-out serviceability as E1 — each tile is replaceable
  • Familiar tile form factor — extends Option C philosophy

Cons

  • Driver must be slim/recessed at 60mm thickness — narrows the supplier set slightly
  • Needs on-site batching area and short curing program (7-day cure)
  • Hot-weather casting protocol in summer (~AED 50-100/m³ added)
  • Surface finish less precise than steel — sealant cycle on tile joints
Substructure cost (mid): AED 2.6M  ·  Per module: ~AED 420  ·  Ground level: Yes (flush)

CEO Recommendation

Two pairings, two purposes

The two extremes of the matrix make the strongest cases. The recommendation is to pick one — either the permanent flagship build or the lower-cost pilot — based on the program's goal.

Permanent Flagship

Option E2 + 16-LED

LWAC concrete tiles (60mm thickness) cast on site, set flat on prepared ground, with the dense 4×4 LED grid. Clean aerial canvas, durable, lowest-cost permanent installation, single-person-lift tiles for service. Each tile is a self-contained module — same philosophy as Option C, simplified for ground level and made locally.

AED ~19.4M
mid-range, all-in  ·  ≈ USD 5.3M  ·  ~AED 3,140 per module
Pilot or Touring

Option D + 9-LED

Modular open-frame grid on prepared ground with the lighter 3×3 LED count. Lowest civil program, fastest install, relocatable. Good fit for a 12-month proof-of-concept.

AED ~13.8M
mid-range, all-in  ·  ≈ USD 3.8M  ·  ~AED 2,235 per module

Avoid: Option C + 16-LED at AED 22.5M is the most expensive pairing — the raised deck no longer offers a meaningful advantage now that E1 (steel tile) and E2 (LWAC tile) exist at lower cost with cleaner ground-level reading and the same lift-out serviceability.

Note on E1 vs E2: E1 (steel tile) is more durable but ~AED 1.1M more than E2 at the same density. E2 is recommended for the flagship if budget matters; E1 is the answer if you want a more "engineered metal" aesthetic and are willing to pay for it.

Walkway

3m glowing glass walkway

A 3m wide, ~17.4m long path runs from the outer edge of the ring to the balloon platform, built from anti-slip laminated tempered glass over an outdoor LED mesh. The path is part of the show and also serves as the operational route for guests, VIPs, and emergency staff.

Next Steps

What needs to happen after the CEO chooses

  1. 01 CEO confirms framing option (C / D / E1 / E2) and density (9-LED / 16-LED).
  2. 02 Issue RFQs to at least 3 suppliers per major line item: LED nodes, controllers, frame extrusion, walkway glass, steel fabricator (E1), LWAC + LECA aggregate (E2).
  3. 03 File PCT patent on the system architecture (sealed modular tile + fixed-stem light pipe + hot-swap tulip head) before any public physical prototype reveal.
  4. 04 If E2: confirm on-site batching/casting area logistics, mold count for parallel batch casting, and hot-weather concreting protocol.
  5. 05 Build Phase A bench prototype (1-4 working tiles) on the chosen framing and density.
  6. 06 Confirm content slate (3-show vs 5-show launch package) — drives the 16-LED vs 9-LED case.
  7. 07 Validate aerial readability with drone or balloon-height calibration before full build-out.
